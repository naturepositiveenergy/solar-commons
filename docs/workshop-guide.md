# Solar Commons: workshop guide

For facilitators who use Solar Commons in a workshop, a class or a stakeholder meeting.

**Status:** reviewed and approved on 6 November 2026. Japanese version: [workshop-guide.ja.md](workshop-guide.ja.md).

## At a glance

- **What players do:** they develop solar parks in a rural Japanese town. Fast, cheap projects make money quickly, but they cost trust and nature, and can lead the town to pass a solar ordinance (条例) that makes every later project harder. Careful projects, with good design and real dialogue with residents, take longer but score better.
- **What participants take away:** why community involvement and nature-friendly design matter for solar in Japan, and which real policies shape these decisions. The policy notebook in the game explains each policy, with official sources.
- **Time:** one game takes 20 to 30 minutes. A full session with introduction and discussion takes 60 to 90 minutes.
- **Group size:** any. Pairs or groups of three per device work best, because players talk about their decisions.
- **What you need:** a phone, tablet or computer with a current browser for each pair, and a projector for yourself. No accounts, no installation and no costs.
- **Languages:** Japanese and English. Each player can switch at any time (top right).

## Before the workshop

1. **Play one game yourself** on the computer you will project from: https://naturepositiveenergy.github.io/solar-commons/
2. **Choose a scenario code.** Everyone who plays the same code gets the same sites and the same events in the same turns, so groups can compare their games afterwards. Any number from 1000 to 9999 works. These three codes have been checked for workshops:

   | Code | Character | Turn 1 sites | Events |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **1052** | Storms and the grid | Reservoir (promotion zone), forest slope, brownfield | Policy change (income −20%) in turn 5, grid delays in turns 7 and 9, **heavy rain in turn 11**, rival developer in turn 14, typhoon in turn 18 |
   | **7870** | Typhoon and rivals | Agrivoltaics (promotion zone), reservoir, brownfield | Curtailment in turn 2, rival developers in turns 10 and 19, **typhoon in turn 11**, policy change (income −20%) in turn 14, grid delay in turn 17 |
   | **1016** | Calm start, for beginners or short sessions | Brownfield, agrivoltaics (promotion zone), farmland | Curtailment in turn 3, grid delay in turn 7 |

   The events are the same for everyone, but their effects differ. For example, heavy rain only damages parks on forest slopes and parks built cheaply. A typhoon only damages parks with fewer than 3 hedges on the edge of the site. This is what makes the discussion interesting.

3. **Prepare the link with your code**, for example https://naturepositiveenergy.github.io/solar-commons/?code=1052. With this link, the start button says "Start scenario 1052". For a QR code, open the link in Chrome on a computer, right-click the page and choose **Create QR code for this page** (in some Chrome versions, this is in the **Share** menu of the address bar instead). Put the link and the QR code on your first slide.
4. **Check the room's Wi-Fi** if you can. The game needs internet only the first time it is opened on a device. After that it also works offline.

## In the room: getting started (about 5 minutes)

1. Show the QR code and the link. Players open it and press **Start scenario 1052** (or the code you chose). Without the link: **Play with a scenario code** on the start screen, then type the code.
2. Ask everyone to check two things:
   - the start screen says **Ready to play offline on this device**, so a weak Wi-Fi no longer matters
   - during the game, **Scenario 1052** appears below the status bar. If a group sees another number, they started a random game. **Title**, then the code again.
3. First-time players get a hint at the top of the screen ("Next step") that guides them through the first park. **How to play** in the top bar explains the rules in six short sections.

## Suggested session plans

**75 minutes**

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Introduction (10 min) | Japan's solar expansion and its conflicts: landslides on forest slopes, landscape, residents who were not asked, local ordinances. The goal of the game. |
| 0:10 | Start (5 min) | Link or QR code, scenario code, "Ready to play offline". |
| 0:15 | Play (30 min) | Walk around. Ask pairs why they chose a site or a design. Remind them of the time at 0:35. |
| 0:45 | Collect results (5 min) | See "Comparing results" below. |
| 0:50 | Discussion (20 min) | Questions below. |
| 1:10 | Close (5 min) | The policy notebook as further reading, the link to keep playing at home. |

**60 minutes:** introduction 5 minutes, discussion 15 minutes, use code **1016**.

**90 minutes:** after the first discussion, play the same scenario again (**Play this scenario again** on the end screen) with a deliberate strategy. For example, half the groups try "fast and cheap" and the other half "careful", then compare the results. A second game takes 20 to 25 minutes, because everyone knows the controls by then.

## Comparing results

- On the end screen, **Copy results** copies a short summary, which players can paste into a chat or a shared document you already use. It looks like this (example values):

  > Solar Commons – Scenario 1052
  > Grade A (Very good), average park score 11.5 of 14
  > Capacity built 22.4 / 15 · Money 63.0 · Trust 68 · Reputation 61
  > Project 1 · Brownfield · 13 of 14: Site selection ○, Nature measures ○, …

- Without a shared document: ask each group for its grade (S, A, B, C or D) and make a tally on the whiteboard. Then ask the groups with the highest and the lowest grade what they did differently.
- If copying doesn't work in a browser, the summary appears in a box below the button, ready to be selected and copied by hand.

## Questions for the discussion

Pick three or four. The policy notebook entry in brackets has the real-world background, with sources. It is open on the start screen and in the game (**Policy notebook**).

**Results**
- What grade did you get? In the same scenario, what did the best and the weakest groups do differently?

**Speed and care**
- Who tried a cheap build or a dense layout? What happened later?
- One group's careless project can lead to an ordinance that makes every later project harder, including careful ones. Who carries the cost of "fast and cheap"? (*Local solar ordinances (条例)*)

**The community**
- Which residents were hardest to win over, and what worked?
- What changed when you held a genuine 説明会 instead of only the formal one required by law? (*Resident briefing (説明会)*)
- Did anyone use a citizen fund? What does it change if local people own part of the park? (*Citizen funds (市民出資)*)

**Nature and design**
- Which nature measures did you use? Why does it matter where hedges and corridors are placed, not just how many? (*Nature-coexistence sites (自然共生サイト)*)
- Why are forest slopes so risky? Who chose one, and what happened in the heavy rain? (*Forest development permit (林地開発許可)*)
- What are the advantages and the difficulties of agrivoltaics? (*Agrivoltaics (営農型太陽光発電)*)

**Same scenario, different outcomes** (codes 1052 and 7870)
- In turn 11, the heavy rain (1052) or the typhoon (7870) hit everyone. Whose parks were damaged, and why not the others?

**From the game to reality**
- Did anyone build in a promotion zone? Why might a town designate one? (*Promotion zones (促進区域)*)
- What would a good solar project in your own town need? Who should be at the table from the start?
- What surprised you?

## Tips

- **Pairs talk more** than single players, and their talk is the most useful part of the workshop.
- **Groups that finish early** can press **Play this scenario again** and try a different strategy.
- **Common reasons players get stuck:**
  - An approved project waits for construction because there isn't enough money. The project card says so. Wait for income, or plan a cheaper park.
  - Residents' support drops during the permit process because nobody talked to them that turn. The hint and the project card warn about this.
  - Money stays below 0 for two turns, or trust falls below 5: the game is lost. That is a good starting point for the discussion too.
- **Games are saved automatically** in the browser. If a page is closed by accident, open the link again and press **Continue game**.
- **Shared devices:** for the next group, press **Title**, then **New game** or the link again. The game asks before it replaces a saved game.
- **On the projector,** zoom with **Ctrl and +** (Mac: **Cmd and +**). The layout adapts to large text.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| The page doesn't load | Check the link and the Wi-Fi. Try another browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox). |
| A group has different sites from the others | Check the scenario code below the status bar. Start again with the link or the code. |
| "Ready to play offline" doesn't appear | Open the game once with internet and wait a few seconds. Private or incognito windows may not keep an offline copy. |
| Copy results doesn't copy | Copy the text from the box that appears below the button. |
| A device shows an older version of the game | Reload the page once with internet. |
| Text is too small to read | Zoom in with Ctrl and + (Cmd and + on a Mac). |

More technical help, including installing the game as an app: [publishing.md](publishing.md).

## About the content

- The game simplifies on purpose: its numbers are game values, not real costs, yields or permit times.
- The policy notebook describes real Japanese policies as of its last review (see [policy-notebook.md](policy-notebook.md)). Policies change. For example, the government has decided to end FIT and FIP support for new ground-mounted solar projects of 10 kW and more from fiscal year 2027, while exceptions (for example for projects in promotion zones) were still being discussed in autumn 2026. The game does not reflect this yet.
- The recommended scenario codes above are for the current version of the game (rules v0.7). If the rules change, they may play differently, and this guide will be updated.
